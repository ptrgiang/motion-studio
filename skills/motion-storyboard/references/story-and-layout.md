# Story and layout

## Beat policy
Use integer frames and half-open ranges. At 30fps a 15s film has 450 frames numbered 0..449; its endpoint is 450. Define cuts at exact boundaries. Define transition ranges inside adjacent shots and allow the renderer to draw both states intentionally. Keep the copy hold visible during transitions only when it remains stable and unobstructed.

## Composition
Choose a grid and key alignment anchors. Make contrast carry hierarchy before motion. Use negative space deliberately. Start text-first, UI-first and diagram-first scenes from different spatial ideas. Do not put every headline in the center. Preserve a clear path from eye-catching detail to proof to next state. Default margins are estimates; consult current destination overlays when a particular platform is requested.

## Format adaptation
16:9: contextual views or two-part relationships may fit.
9:16: stack information, enlarge detail crop, reduce simultaneous UI panels and shorten copy.
1:1: re-balance type and anchors; choose holds based on readability, never automatically shorten them.
Specify safe insets per format and target platform; keep important text, logo and CTA inside them. Do not confuse an encoder's resolution with usable safe area. Check on a phone-sized image.

## Optional 15-second example
0–2s: visible friction; 2–5s: one constrained state; 5–9s: actual interaction; 9–12s: truthful proof; 12–15s: stable CTA. Treat these times as an example, not an obligatory template.

## Styleframe record
Name frame, format, intended focal object, copy, asset ids and layout rationale. A still establishes composition; it cannot validate an entrance, a spring, a cut or audio sync.
